# Task: guiding questions for one section

You are given one section of Scripture in two translations (BSB and KJV) and the key ideas the
commentators make about it, each with its ID. Write up to 8 candidate questions. Code chooses the
final pool from them, so offer a spread: different key ideas and different reaches. Follow the
question guide below.

## What a question is for

A question guides the reader into the passage's meaning before they see what the commentators
saw. It is never a reading quiz. Start from something the key ideas show to be significant: a
surprising detail, a tension, an unusual order of events, a word that carries weight, a link to
what came before. Point the reader to it and ask what they make of it.

- Usually one short framing sentence that points at the detail, in your own words, then the
  question: "Mark stops to describe John's clothes and food. Why might those details matter?"
- Never ask the reader to repeat what the text says: no "What does verse 6 say…", "What two
  things…", "How does John describe…", "Who…", "Where…", or any question a reader answers by
  copying words from the passage.
- Aim at a key idea (`targets`: the idea IDs) without giving it away. The framing may name the
  detail; it must not state the point the commentators draw from it.
- Answerable by thinking about the passage alone, with no commentary in front of the reader.
- Open-ended, one question, at most 160 characters in all, ending with "?".
- Not leading, and assuming no one tradition's doctrine.
- Quoting: if you put a word or phrase in quotation marks, use single quotation marks ('like
  this'), never double ones. A double quotation mark ends the answer's text field early.
- Doesn't quote wording found in only one of the two translations. Describe it, or use words both
  share. To point to a verse, write `{{ref:C:V}}` or `{{ref:C:V-V}}`.

## Reach

Questions differ in how far they reach, and code sets each question's level from that:
- **A detail**: one or two verses, nothing needed beyond the passage. The reader's first
  question on the section.
- **The passage**: the whole section, or a contrast across several verses. Unlocks a day later.
- **Beyond it**: connects the passage to the rest of Scripture, to history, or to the reader's own
  life. Unlocks after that.

Include at least two questions on a single detail, at least one on the whole section, and, when
there are three or more key ideas, at least one that reaches beyond it.

## What to record

- `type`: `noticing` (points at a detail and asks what the reader makes of it), `meaning` (what
  the passage or a contrast in it means), or `application` (connecting it to the rest of
  Scripture or to life).
- `verseStart`, `verseEnd`: the verses the question is about, inside the section. Use the whole
  section for a question about the passage as a whole.
- `answerDirectness`: `stated` (the detail the reader thinks about is plainly there), `inferred`
  (the reader must reason it out), or `beyond` (it reaches past the text).
- `outsideKnowledge`: `none`, `some` (a little background helps), or `required` (it needs other
  Scripture, history, or the reader's own life).
