# Task: an introduction to one book

You are given each commentator's preface to one book of the Bible. Write the short introduction a
reader sees once, as the first post before the book's first passage. It sets the scene so each
passage's own notes can stay about that passage.

## What to write (`text`)

80 to 120 words, in plain modern English, as a thoughtful friend would explain it:
- who wrote the book, and what is known about them;
- to whom, and when and where, as far as the commentators say;
- why it was written;
- what to watch for while reading it: what is distinctive about this book.

Say only what the commentators say in their prefaces. Where they are unsure or disagree (a date,
an authorship tradition), say so plainly rather than choosing. Don't name the commentators in the
text: the app shows the sources beside it. Don't quote either translation of the Bible.

## Sources and evidence

- `sources`: every commentator whose preface supports the introduction, by id:
  `matthew-henry`, `jamieson-fausset-brown`, `adam-clarke`.
- `evidence`: for every source, one quote of 8 to 30 words copied exactly, word for word, from
  that commentator's preface, showing what they say. Readers see these quotes under "In their
  words", so choose a whole sentence or clause that reads well on its own: not a list of
  references and not a fragment. A source you can't quote must be left out.
