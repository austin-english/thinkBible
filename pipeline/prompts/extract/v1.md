# Task: key ideas, context and "views differ" for one section

You are given one section of Scripture in two translations (BSB and KJV) and each commentator's
notes on it. Some notes are labelled "Parallel passage note": that commentator wrote their note on
a parallel passage (often in another Gospel) and points readers to it from here. Treat those
notes as the commentator's comment on this section's passage.

## Key ideas (1 to 5)

A key idea is a point the commentators make about this passage that a careful reader could notice
or reason out from the passage itself.

- Restate only what the commentators say. Do not add interpretations, facts, or cross-references
  they don't give, and do not choose between them where they disagree.
- Write in plain, warm, modern English for an adult reader, not in the commentators' 18th- and
  19th-century style.
- Don't quote either translation's wording. Describe what the verses say in your own words. To
  point to a verse, write a placeholder: `{{ref:C:V}}` for one verse or `{{ref:C:V-V}}` for a
  range, using this section's chapter and verse numbers.
- `title`: at most 60 characters. `body`: one or two sentences, at most 240 characters.
- `sources`: every commentator who makes this point, by id: `matthew-henry`,
  `jamieson-fausset-brown`, `adam-clarke`. When two or three make the same point, list them all
  on one idea rather than writing near-duplicate ideas.
- `evidence`: for every commentator in `sources`, one quote of 4 to 25 words copied exactly, word
  for word, from that commentator's notes above. The quote must show that this commentator makes
  this point. A source you can't quote must be left out.
- `verseStart` and `verseEnd`: the verses this idea is about, inside this section. Use the whole
  section when the commentator speaks about the passage as a whole.
- Order: the most important and best-supported ideas first. Give fewer ideas rather than weak
  ones, but don't leave out a point two or more commentators make.

## Context (`context`)

Two short lines, at most 280 characters: who wrote this book, to whom, and why, as it bears on
this section. Use what the commentators say; where they say nothing on it, keep to what is
common ground.

## Views differ (`viewsDiffer`)

Where the commentators, or the traditions they mention, read this passage differently, describe
the difference neutrally in at most 400 characters, without taking a side. Otherwise null.

## No prompt (`noPrompt`)

Set `noPrompt` to true only when the commentators give nothing a reader could be asked about
from the passage itself, for example a bare list of names they don't discuss. Decide this from
the commentary, never from the kind of passage: genealogies, lists and laws still get key ideas
wherever the commentators discuss them. When true, give a short `noPromptReason`; key ideas are
still welcome if the commentators make any point.
