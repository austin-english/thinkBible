# Question guide

The commentary decides what is worth asking about; the passage is what the reader answers from.
A question guides the reader into what the passage means. It is never a quiz on what they read.
These questions set ThinkBible's voice.

## Quality rules

- Guiding, not testing: start from something significant in the passage (a surprising detail, a
  tension, an unusual order, a word that carries weight) and ask what the reader makes of it.
  Never ask the reader to repeat what the text says.
- Open-ended: never a yes-or-no question.
- Short: usually one framing sentence and one question.
- Answerable by thinking about the passage itself.
- Not leading: the framing may point at the detail, but never states the point the commentators
  draw from it.
- No assumptions about one tradition's doctrine. On contested passages keep the question open
  ("What does this passage say about God's choosing?").
- Works in either translation: describe what the verses say rather than quoting wording only one
  translation uses ("Holy Spirit" in one, "Holy Ghost" in the other).
- Shaped by the passage: in a story ask why people act as they do; in a letter about the
  argument; in poetry about the images; in prophecy about the promise or warning. In genealogies,
  lists and laws, ask about the verses the commentators single out.
- Warm and curious, as a friend reading alongside.

## Reach (sets the level)

- **Level 1, a detail**: one or two verses, nothing needed beyond the passage. The first question
  a reader meets on a section, so it must be easy to start on and still worth thinking about.
- **Level 2, the passage**: the whole section, or a contrast across several verses.
- **Level 3, beyond it**: connects to the rest of Scripture, to history, or to the reader's life.

## Avoid: quiz questions

These test whether the reader read the verses rather than help them understand them:
- What two things does Mark say about Jesus in the very first sentence of his gospel?
- What does verse 6 tell us about John's clothing and diet?
- How does John describe the One who is coming after him?
- What three things happen as Jesus comes up out of the water?

## Examples (Mark 1)

Adapted from the Mark 1 designs. Where a translation's wording differs, the BSB form is shown with
the KJV one after it.

Level 1 (a detail)
1. Mark tells you who Jesus is in his very first line, before a single story. Why might he want
   you to know that from the start? (1:1)
2. Mark stops to describe John's clothes and food. Why might those details have mattered to his
   first readers? (1:6)
3. Why do you think John says he isn't worthy to untie the straps of Jesus's sandals? (1:7)
   KJV: Why do you think John says he isn't worthy to unloose Jesus's shoes?
4. Mark says the heavens were opened as Jesus came up. What do you think that picture is telling
   us? (1:10)
5. Right after God calls Jesus his beloved Son, the Spirit drives him into the wilderness. What do
   you make of that timing? (1:11-12)
6. Why might Mark mention the wild animals and the angels? (1:13)
7. Jesus puts "repent" and "believe" together. What do you think each one means here? (1:15)

Level 2 (the passage)
8. Mark starts his gospel with John in the wilderness, not with Jesus's birth. Why do you think he
   begins here? (1:1-8)
9. Mark quotes Isaiah before he tells us anything about John. What does that say about John's
   role? (1:2-4)
10. John baptizes with water, but says Jesus will baptize with the Holy Spirit. What difference is
    he pointing to? (1:7-8, with the scene that follows)
    KJV: … but says Jesus will baptize with the Holy Ghost. …
11. Jesus starts preaching only after John is arrested. Why do you think Mark connects the two?
    (1:12-15)

Level 3 (beyond it)
12. God says he is pleased with Jesus before Jesus has done any public ministry. Why do you think
    that matters? (1:11)
13. The Son, the Spirit and the Father all appear in this scene. What does that tell you about who
    Jesus is? (1:9-11)
14. Forty days in the wilderness may remind readers of Israel's forty years. What connection do you
    see? (1:12-13)
15. Simon and Andrew leave their nets at once to follow Jesus. What might leaving your nets look
    like for you? (1:16-18) (new, not from the designs)
