# Question guide

The commentary decides what is worth asking about; the passage is what the reader answers from.
These questions set ThinkBible's voice.

## Quality rules

- Open-ended: never a yes-or-no question.
- Short: one question at a time, one or two plain sentences. A short framing sentence before the
  question is fine ("Mark quotes Isaiah before he tells us anything about John.").
- Answerable from the passage itself.
- Not leading: don't put the answer in the question or hint at the "right" reading.
- No assumptions about one tradition's doctrine. On contested passages keep the question open
  ("What does this passage say about God's choosing?").
- Aimed at a key idea without giving it away.
- Works in either translation: describe what the verses say rather than quoting wording only one
  translation uses ("Holy Spirit" in one, "Holy Ghost" in the other).
- Shaped by the passage: in a story ask about what people do and why; in a letter about the
  argument; in poetry about the images; in prophecy about the promise or warning. In genealogies,
  lists and laws, ask about the verses the commentators single out.
- Warm and curious, as a friend reading alongside, never a quiz or a test.

## Levels

- **Level 1, notice**: one or two verses, the answer nearly stated, no outside knowledge, often a
  point all three commentators agree on.
- **Level 2, interpret**: several verses or a contrast, the answer must be reasoned out, a little
  context helps.
- **Level 3, connect**: needs other Scripture, history, or life application; often a deeper
  point from one commentator.

## Examples (Mark 1)

From the Mark 1 designs. Where the designs had a wording for each translation, the BSB one is
shown with the KJV one after it.

Level 1 (notice)
1. How does John describe the One who is coming after him? (1:7-8)
2. What three things happen as Jesus comes up out of the water? (1:10-11)

Level 2 (interpret)
3. Mark starts his gospel with John in the wilderness, not with Jesus's birth. Why do you think he
   begins here? (the whole passage, 1:1-8)
4. Mark quotes Isaiah before he tells us anything about John. What does that say about John's
   role? (1:2-4)
5. Why do you think John says he isn't worthy to untie the straps of Jesus's sandals? (1:7)
   KJV: Why do you think John says he isn't worthy to unloose Jesus's shoes?
6. John baptizes with water, but says Jesus will baptize with the Holy Spirit. What difference is
   he pointing to? (1:8)
   KJV: … but says Jesus will baptize with the Holy Ghost. …
7. Mark says the heavens were opened as Jesus came up. What do you think that picture is telling
   us? (1:10)
   BSB: Mark says the heavens were breaking open. Why might he use such a strong picture?
8. Right after God calls Jesus his beloved Son, the Spirit drives him into the wilderness. Why do
   you think those two moments sit side by side? (1:9-13)
9. Why might Mark mention the wild animals and the angels? (1:13)
10. Jesus starts preaching right after John is arrested. Why do you think Mark connects the two?
    (1:14-15)
11. Jesus puts "repent" and "believe" together. What do you think each one means here? (1:15)

Level 3 (connect)
12. God says he is pleased with Jesus before Jesus has done any public ministry. Why do you think
    that matters? (1:11)
13. The Son, the Spirit and the Father all appear in this scene. What does that tell you about who
    Jesus is? (1:9-11)
14. Forty days in the wilderness may remind readers of Israel's forty years. What connection do you
    see? (1:12-13)
15. Simon and Andrew leave their nets at once to follow Jesus. What might leaving your nets look
    like for you? (1:16-18) (new, not from the designs)
