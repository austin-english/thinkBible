# Question guide

The commentary decides what is worth asking about; the passage is where the reader starts. A
question helps the reader uncover what lies beneath the surface of the passage: what it shows
about God, about Jesus, and about following him. It is never a quiz on what they read. These
questions set ThinkBible's voice. (Approved by the owner, 2026-11-17, from a rating of 51 Mark 1
questions.)

## Quality rules

- Beneath the surface: aim at a meaning the commentators draw out, which a casual reading would
  miss. A detail is worth asking about when it opens one of those meanings.
- Approachable: the reader can make a real start from the passage, plus one plain fact if they
  need it. The reply takes them the rest of the way.
- One concrete handle, one simple ask: point at one thing in the text (a contrast, a choice, an
  order of events, a weighty word) and ask what it reveals.
- Stands alone: readers see each question on its own, a week apart, so it names its people.
- Plain: the way a friend reading alongside would ask it out loud. Short; one question.
- Open-ended: never a yes-or-no question.
- Not leading: the opening sentence says what happens and never states the meaning.
- No assumptions about one church tradition's doctrine where Christian traditions differ. Where
  the commentators disagree, keep the question open.
- Works in either translation: describe what the verses say in plain modern words rather than
  quoting wording only one translation uses (the KJV's "Holy Ghost"; plain "Holy Spirit" is
  modern English, not a quotation).
- Shaped by the passage: in a story ask why people act as they do; in a letter about the
  argument; in poetry about the images; in prophecy about the promise or warning.
- Warm and curious, as a friend reading alongside.

## Avoid: quiz questions

These test whether the reader read the verses rather than help them understand them:
- What two things does Mark say about Jesus in the very first sentence of his gospel?
- What does verse 6 tell us about John's clothing and diet?
- How does John describe the One who is coming after him?

## Questions the owner loved (Mark 1)

These set the voice. Notice the shape: one concrete thing from the passage, then a short, plain
question about what it reveals.

- "John baptizes with water but says Jesus will baptize with the Holy Spirit. What difference is
  he pointing to?"
- "John distinguishes his water baptism from what the one coming after him will bring. What does
  that distinction say about the limits of what John's ministry can accomplish?"
- "Jesus says he will make the fishermen 'fishers of men.' What does the image of fishing add to
  what following him will mean?"
- "Jesus calls people to repent and to believe. What would be missing if someone did only one of
  those things?"
- "Jesus calls fishermen at their nets, not teachers or priests. Why might he begin with men like
  these?"
- "Jesus calls people in the middle of ordinary work. What might that suggest about who he is
  looking for?"
- "Following Jesus costs these four men their trade and their family ties on the spot. What does
  that say about what Jesus was asking of them?"
- "Jesus went through temptation himself before calling anyone to follow him. Why might that order
  matter for the people who would later come to him with their own struggles?"
- "Jesus heals Simon's mother-in-law, and the first thing she does is serve her guests. Why might
  Mark include her serving alongside the fact of her healing?"
- "Peter's mother-in-law is healed and immediately serves. What would be missing if she had been
  healed but stayed in bed?"
- "Jesus drives out many demons but will not let them speak. Why might he silence those who,
  among everyone present, actually recognized who he was?"
